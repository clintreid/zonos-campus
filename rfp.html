The process

The request for proposal

Tech Ridge Management, working with and on behalf of Zonos, issued a request for proposal on August 24, 2026 for full architectural and design services for the Zonos Office Campus. This page summarizes what it asks for. Responses are due September 25, and the design team gets chosen after proposals, site visits, and interviews.

Scope

What the design team takes on

DisciplineCovers
Site planningVehicular and pedestrian circulation, parking layout (surface, below grade, and structured), grading and drainage coordination, utilities, and compliance with Tech Ridge site standards.
Landscape architectureThe entire site: all open space between buildings, the connector between the two parcels, plaza and courtyard areas, streetscape, and setback landscaping.
Building architectureFull architectural design of all buildings across both phases and the optional retail phase, including massing, facades, base building systems, and parking coordination.
Tenant improvement and designCore-and-shell interiors: lobbies, corridors, restrooms, interior design standards, and tenant improvement guidelines.

The scope covers both parcels: the 4.04-acre headquarters parcel and the 0.88-acre hangar parcel beside it, including the area that connects them. Two facts anchor everything: the office cap is 200,000 gross square feet across both phases, and both office buildings must sit along the rim edge on the east side of the property. The RFP calls the rim siting one of the most important design constraints in the scope.

The design team is asked to draw both phase 2 options, so the garage decision can wait until the parking question resolves.

The existing container park and Hangar C appear in the document as material and design-quality references.

Design guidelines

The zone plan the design answers to

The RFP carries the Tech Ridge Zone Plan architectural design guidelines as its appendix. The short version: contemporary architecture; simplicity of forms; varied massing and rooflines; pedestrian-scaled ground floors; durable, high-quality materials suited to the southern Utah climate; a cool-tone base palette with warm natural accents drawn from the surrounding landscape; and no continuous uninterrupted facades. A developer review committee approves designs first, then the city reviews them through the permit process.

The tension

The guidelines describe contemporary architecture and note a lack of ornamentation as characteristic; the Zonos exploration keeps reaching for ornament, character, and the question "why don't people build like this anymore." The guidelines also say they encourage innovation rather than prescribe a style, which is the room the design team gets to work in. Resolving this, credibly, through the review committee, is part of the architect's job.
